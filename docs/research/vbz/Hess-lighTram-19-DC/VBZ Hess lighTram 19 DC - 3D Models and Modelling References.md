# VBZ Hess lighTram 19 DC (battery trolleybus 200–229, prototype SwissTrolley plus 183) – 3D Models and Modelling References

The **Hess lighTram® 19 DC** is an 18.74 m, three-axle articulated **battery trolleybus** (Hess type **BGT-N2D**, ABB electrics). "DC" stands for Hess's *Dynamic Charging*: the bus charges its LTO traction battery from the overhead wires while it runs and covers wire-free sections on battery. Hess also sold the same body as **Swisstrolley 5** or **SwissTrolley 19 DC** (Bern, Biel). VBZ runs three series: **200–209** (2020–2022), **210–222** (2024–2025) and **223–229** (2026). That makes 30 buses. Their forerunner is the one-off **SwissTrolley plus no. 183** (2016/2017), which has its own section below. The first series replaced diesel buses on line 83 in spring 2020. Wires on line 69 went up in August 2025, but trolleybus service there only started on 19 October 2026 because the later buses arrived late. This note belongs to [[VBZ Bus Fleet 2026 - Overview]]. The older articulated trolleybus is in [[VBZ Hess Swisstrolley 4 - 3D Models and Modelling References]] and the double-articulated sister is in [[VBZ Hess lighTram 25 DC - 3D Models and Modelling References]].

> [!warning] Look-alikes and variants
> - **The three VBZ series look the same from outside.** The differences are technical. VBZ lists **200–209** with 2 × 160 kW, a 66 kWh battery, 60 km/h and a heat pump with air conditioning. It lists **210–222** with 2 × 230 kW, 61 kWh, 70 km/h and a **CO2 heat pump**. Both have 117 places (34 seats). Expect small roof-equipment differences (HVAC unit), but I found **no photo of a 21x or 22x unit** on Commons to check. VBZ publishes no data for 223–229 yet.
> - **Livery:** standard VBZ blue and white. No. **200** wore a green "Der 83er fährt elektrisch" wrap on the upper body when new (photo-02). It ran its 2020 test trips in a neutral livery ([Urban Transport Magazine](https://www.urban-transport-magazine.com/zuerich-die-linie-83-faehrt-jetzt-elektrisch/)).
> - **SwissTrolley plus 183** is the 2016 prototype. It is the same length but **3.45 m** high (series buses: 3.50 m), has 2 × 152 kW central motors and a 60 kWh battery. It still carries its own green/blue/white "swisstrolleyplus.ch" livery (photos below).
> - **[[VBZ Hess Swisstrolley 4 - 3D Models and Modelling References|Swisstrolley 4]]** (162–182, 2013, Vossloh Kiepe electrics) is the older body generation: 18.7 × 3.45 m, 2 × 120 kW and only a small 75 kW emergency battery. Both run on the same lines, so read the fleet number before you use a street photo (162–182 = Swisstrolley 4, 200–229 = lighTram 19 DC).
> - **Other lighTram 19 DC / Swisstrolley 5 trolleybuses** have the same body: **St. Gallen VBSG** (101–), **Bern** (Swisstrolley 5, 21–28 from 2017), **Biel** (10 units), **Lausanne TL** (802–827 on Commons), **Neuchâtel transN**, **Salzburg**, **Lyon TCL**. Lucerne VBL is also getting lighTram 19 DCs. I only saw that in search-result snippets (a 2024 VVL strategy PDF: "ab 2024: 30 Batterie-Trolleybusse von Hess AG"), so it is not checked. Liveries and door or seat layouts differ.
> - **Not trolleybuses:** the **lighTram 19 TOSA** (Geneva TPG, flash charging at stops, no poles), the **lighTram 19 OPP** (Bern, Lugano, pantograph charging) and the **lighTram 19 Plug** (depot charging, 18.7 m) share the body shell but have different roofs and no trolley poles.
> - **lighTram 25 DC** (24.7 m, double-articulated) is the long sister. See [[VBZ Hess lighTram 25 DC - 3D Models and Modelling References]].

## Free game assets (closest thing to a download)

### Transport Fever 3 (mod.io): purpose-built lighTram 19 DC
- [VBSG HESS lighTram 19 & 25 DC](https://mod.io/g/transportfever3/m/vbsg-hess-lightram-19-25-dc) by AlbertSchweizer. It went live on 16 Nov 2026 and is 2.62 MB (PC), with **2,406 subscribers** and 2,613 downloads when I checked. It contains St. Gallen lighTram 19 DC **no. 102** and lighTram 25 DC no. 134, with an interior, moving joints, animated doors and night lights. The author says the models were built "using HESS documentation and photos of the VBSG fleet". **This is the only lighTram 19 DC model I found.** It is in VBSG red and white, so you'd have to repaint it in VBZ colours. It is listed in the mod.io [Switzerland collection](https://mod.io/g/transportfever3/c/switzerland). You need Transport Fever 3, and the mesh is in game format.

### Cities: Skylines (Steam Workshop): REV0's generic "Swiss Trolley 2019"
REV0's Hess set is free. You need Steam and Cities: Skylines (app 255710). The meshes are low-poly "Polygon line coloured" `.crp` assets that share one texture set. The preview image says **"Swisstrolley 5 Articulated"**, so this is the same body generation, in generic colours. They are part of the [HESS AG collection](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1235954526).

## Prototype: SwissTrolley plus no. 183 (2016)
Hess developed no. 183 with VBZ, ETH Zürich and the Bern University of Applied Sciences, in a lighthouse project backed by the Swiss Federal Office of Energy (BFE). ABB supplied the electrics. VBZ presented it in January 2017 ([vbzonline.ch interview, 17 Jan 2017](https://vbzonline.ch/hier-fahrt-die-zukunft/)). The trial ran from 2017 to 2019. A DC/DC converter decouples the drive from the overhead line, so braking energy can always go into the battery. Adapted heating and cooling cut energy use by about 15 %. In service it covers about 10 km on battery, and about 35 km on empty runs ([Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hess_Swisstrolley#Swisstrolley_plus), [trolley:motion, June 2026](https://trolleymotion.eu/story/zurich-warum-imc-in-einer-wachsenden-stadt-funktioniert/)). It was tested in other cities too, including Winterthur on 10–19 April 2018 (bus-bild.de captions below). It ran on the new line 83 from early 2020, next to the series buses. VBZ's vehicle page and the Wikipedia fleet table both still list 183 in Nov 2026.

Modelling differences from the series buses: 3.45 m instead of 3.50 m high (compare its roof housings with photo-02 before you model the roof), its own livery, and two central motors instead of the series buses' axle motors. You can't see the motors from outside. Commons has **no photo of 183**. The two photos below are copyrighted bus-bild.de photos, saved for personal reference only.

> [!tip] Suggested modelling workflow
> 1. Put `VBZ-lighTram19DC-Hess-side-render-transN.png` in Blender as the side background and scale the body to **18.74 m** (about 8.9 mm/px). Put the floor plan under it for the plan view, matching the wheel arches and doors. Use the front and rear renders for the cross-section: 2.55 m wide, 3.50 m over the roof equipment.
> 2. Block out the body as two sections joined by the bellows. Axles are at about 2.71 m, 8.57 m and 15.31 m from the front. Check the door spacing against the Rietze artwork. Doors on the right: 1 single + 3 double.
> 3. Model the front from photo-01, 03, 09 and 14, and the rear from the rear render, photo-11 and photo-17. Use photo-11 and the Hess renders for the roof housings and the stowed trolley poles.
> 4. Pick the variant early. For a 200–209 bus, use VBZ blue and white (or no. 200's green 2020 wrap, photo-02). For a 210–229 bus the outside is probably the same, but there are no photos to confirm the roof HVAC unit. For **183**, lower the roof housings to 3.45 m and use the swisstrolleyplus.ch livery.
> 5. If you own Transport Fever 3, load AlbertSchweizer's VBSG lighTram 19 DC to check the volumes. It is the only purpose-built lighTram 19 DC mesh available.
